# Population-scale daily schedules resolve activity context in mobility-based segregation

Mobility-based segregation is commonly measured from the demographic composition of people who visit a location. We show that pooling people present at a location for different activity purposes and at different times can only understate location segregation, whereas estimating detailed purpose- and time-specific contexts from few observations can raise and destabilize estimates. Resolving activity context therefore requires both behavioral detail and population coverage. Yet no single data source provides both: household travel surveys contain activity-purpose and demographic detail but observe few people in any detailed context, whereas passively collected mobility data provide population-scale flows without either. We combine the two by linking survey-based demographic and activity relationships to aggregate origin--destination--time flows through shared latent classes to reconstruct population-scale daily schedules. Applied to Singapore and Seoul, the framework recovers withheld age- and purpose-stratified mobility patterns (\(R^2 = 0.72\)--\(0.91\) for origin--destination flows) and remains robust across latent-class resolutions and perturbations to both data sources. Resolving purpose and time reveals segregation concealed by location-only measurement, concentrated in particular activities and hours: work contributes 38--48\% of this concealed segregation accumulated over the day, reflecting both the distinct composition of work contexts and the long hours spent in them. Reconstructed population-scale schedules thus allow activity context to be incorporated into mobility-based segregation measurement.
